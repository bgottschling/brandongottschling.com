> **Why**
> Every skill is a gift. When a local church needs a digital home that actually reflects who they are, that is a chance to serve with purpose. This project is me using the craft God gave me to help a growing community reach more people.

## The Church

180 Life Church is a warm, non-denominational community in Bloomfield, Connecticut. Their doors are open to everyone. The heart behind 180 Life is simple: know God, find real connection, and take the next step together. They are building something life-giving, and their website should feel like that.

## The Work

A ground-up rebuild. Modern, accessible, mobile-first, fast, and a visual identity that communicates welcome before a single word is read. Just as important: the people who run the church need to run the site without me on the phone.

## Where We Are

The site went live in May 2026 and has run ever since without a major incident. Six months in, it has been a huge success.

It belongs to the church now. Over the summer the code and the hosting moved into the church’s own accounts. The staff keep the content current in the CMS, sermons arrive from the church’s YouTube channel, and events and contact messages flow through Planning Center. I still take on code changes when they need something new. In November that was a background video for the homepage hero, plus a seasonal mode: an announcement can take over the hero for something like Christmas and hand it back on its own when its dates end.

Since launch I have also:

- Run a security pass: rich text is sanitized, links are validated, security headers are in place, and personal staff emails stay off the public site.
- Added an inline audio player to sermon cards, for listening on the go.
- Made the giving page editable in the CMS and split leadership into a staff team and a support team.
- Moved the video library to the YouTube Data API after YouTube’s old feed endpoint stopped working.

There were a few bumps. We came close to the hosting plan’s limits, so I lengthened how long pages and images stay cached and let the CMS’s publish webhook keep pages fresh. A couple of regressions slipped onto content pages, such as rich text losing its line breaks and an announcement that wouldn’t publish. Each one was fixed, and none took the site down.

## Stack

- **Core:** Next.js, Tailwind, Vercel
- **Content:** A CMS the staff edit directly; sermons from the YouTube Data API; events and contact workflows through Planning Center
- **Hosting:** Incremental revalidation, refreshed by a publish webhook from the CMS
